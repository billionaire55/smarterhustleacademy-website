Why small choices matter

Money decisions have an opportunity cost: choosing one use of a dollar means giving up another use. A purchase can be worth it, but a learner should be able to explain what the dollar is doing today and what future goal it delays.

Illustrative context: A $15 weekly habit equals about $780 over 52 weeks. That is not a prediction of what any investment will earn; it is a reminder that frequency and consistency make small amounts visible.

Where Did My Money Go?

Have you ever had money and then wondered — wait, where did it all go? Money usually doesn't disappear. It leaves in small pieces. Today you'll learn how to control where it goes.

J
Jordan's $80 Weekend

Jordan helps his uncle clean out a garage and earns $80. By Sunday: pizza $16, game add-on $12, snacks $8, phone case $20, convenience trip $11 — $67 spent. Jordan has $13 left and says, "How did I spend almost all of it?" Nothing mysterious happened — Jordan just spent without a plan first.

Three Jobs for Your Money

  • SPEND — money you use now (food, games, clothes)
  • SAVE — money you keep for later (laptop, car, college)
  • GROW — money you use to try to make more money (investing, a small business)
📊 Diagram — Give Every Dollar a Job
A simple starting split — spend on what you need, save for what's next, grow for your future.

How Can I Make Money?

Before you can manage money, there's another question: where does it come from? Money usually starts with solving a problem for someone.

Chapter 3 of 12

Spend It or Save It?

Saving doesn't mean never having fun, and spending doesn't mean you're bad with money. The goal is balance.

C
Chris Wants New Sneakers

Chris has $150. Sneakers cost $140. He's also saving for a $400 gaming system. Buying the sneakers leaves only $10. Chris's choices: buy now, wait and save more, or find a cheaper pair.

The 24-Hour Rule

For something you want but don't need: wait 24 hours, then decide. You may still want it — or you may realize you don't.

Cartoon Money Moment
Chris: They're only $140! Friend: You only have $150.
Chris: Why are you bringing math into this?
Before buying, ask: Do I need it? Do I really want it? Can I afford it? Will buying it hurt another goal?

Chapter 5 of 12

Banks Without the Confusion

Banks are tools. You don't need every banking term — just what the basic tools do.

J
Jordan's First Bank Account

Jordan opens his first account with a parent and deposits $300. He spends $45 with his debit card. New balance: $255. The debit card didn't give him extra money — it used money he already had.

The Basics

  • Checking = everyday spending money
  • Savings = money you're keeping
  • Debit card = money leaves your account immediately
  • ATM = a machine to withdraw or deposit cash
Cartoon Money Moment
Jordan swipes debit card. Jordan: Free money!
Bank app: Absolutely not.
Never: share your PIN, share passwords, give strangers account info, click suspicious bank links, or send money because someone pressures you.

Credit Isn't Free Money

Here's the most important credit lesson: credit is borrowed money. It is not extra income. You have to repay it.

C
Chris Buys Headphones

Chris wants $200 headphones and uses credit. Now he owes $200 — and if interest is added because the balance is carried, he could repay more than $200.

Cartoon Money Moment

Simple credit rules: pay on time, don't borrow more than you can afford, don't treat your limit like a spending goal, read the terms.

Chapter 10 of 12

Can You Spot the Scam?

Scammers want you to act before you think. They use fear, pressure, excitement, and urgency. Your job is to slow down.

C
Chris Gets a Message

"Congratulations! You won $5,000. Pay a $50 fee to receive your prize." Chris never entered a contest. Red flags: unexpected money, payment required, urgency, unknown sender.

The Four-Step Scam Check

STOP — don't rush. CHECK — look carefully. VERIFY — contact the real organization another way. DECIDE — only act after checking.

Cartoon Money Moment
Message: YOU WON $1 MILLION!!! Chris: I didn't enter anything.
Message: Please stop asking smart questions.

Could You Make Your Own Money?

A business begins when someone solves a problem for someone else and gets paid — it can start with one skill, one customer, one sale.

M
Maya's Flyer Business

Maya offers $20 digital flyers and gets 5 customers: $20 × 5 = $100 revenue. Costs total $20. $100 − $20 = $80 profit.

Revenue = money coming in. Expenses = money spent. Profit = Revenue − Expenses.
