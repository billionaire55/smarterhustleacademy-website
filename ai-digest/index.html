AI Got Cheaper. Here's Your Next Business Move.

Your Weekly Blueprint for AI, Capital & Hustle

Week of September 23–29, 2026 · By Davette Anderson · 6-minute read

You're probably paying for AI tools right now that aren't paying you back. This week, the big AI companies made that problem easier to fix — and easier to make worse.

They pushed hard on two fronts: AI that keeps working in the background, and models that do more for less money. The question for you is simple: which upgrade actually earns its cost in your business?

1 · The Big Story

Always-on AI enters the business workflow

What happened: On September 29, at its DevDay event, OpenAI introduced dots — AI agents powered by GPT-6 Astra. Each one runs on its own cloud computer, connects to your apps, and keeps working toward a goal you set, even when you're not in the chat.

One example OpenAI gave: turning an interview transcript into suggested clips, show notes, and social posts, all waiting for your approval. OpenAI also says plainly that agents make mistakes and important work still needs a human check.

Who can use it: The rollout starts with Pro and Business Premium users in eligible markets. Enterprise customers get a beta that an admin has to switch on. If you're on a free or Plus plan, you can't use it yet — and that's fine. Start planning now.

Why it matters for your money

Most small business owners don't lose hours on big projects. They lose them on the small, repeated stuff: follow-ups, summaries, reposting, checking in. Always-on agents go after exactly that work.

But a subscription only earns its place if the finished work is usable. If you spend an hour fixing what the AI did, you didn't save an hour.

What this means for you

  • Business owners: Pick one recurring task — like a weekly summary of customer feedback — and test it before you automate anything bigger.
  • Creators: Try turning one piece of content into several marketing pieces, then review every one before it goes out.
  • Investors: Watch for paying customers and repeat use, not launch-day buzz.

The SHA takeaway: Measure time saved after corrections. That's the only number that matters.

2 · Top Upgrades & Tool Releases

Three developments worth testing

GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI) — near-top performance at a fraction of the price

Released September 29. OpenAI says Sol comes close to its top model, GPT-6 Astra, on coding, computer use, and professional work — at about one-fifth of Astra's API price ($2 per million input tokens, $10 per million output).

Try this: Give Sol and your current model the same business task and compare the results. Note: this price cut is for developers using the API. It doesn't lower your ChatGPT subscription.

Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Anthropic) — faster work, lower reported cost

Released September 28. Anthropic says it runs 30% faster than Sonnet 5 and costs up to 30% less per task, at the same $2 / $10 per-million-token price.

Try this: Run a writing task you do often. Compare how long it takes, how much you have to edit, and what it actually costs you. These are the company's numbers, so check them against your own work.

ChatGPT Space (OpenAI) — one shared place for a project

Announced September 29. Space puts your teammates, your files, and AI in one shared workspace. It's available on Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans (desktop and web; mobile is view-and-share only).

Try this: Keep your launch brief, product details, and campaign drafts in one Space so everyone — including any contractor you hire — works from the same information.

3 · Capital & Investments

The Money Trail: where the money is moving

AMD agrees to buy World Labs for about $8.2 billion

AMD announced the all-stock deal on September 28. It's expected to close by year-end, pending regulators. World Labs, founded by AI researcher Fei-Fei Li, builds "spatial AI" that creates and simulates interactive 3D worlds — useful for robotics and simulation.

Investor takeaway: An announced deal is not a finished one, and it's not proven profit. Watch for share dilution (all-stock deals create new shares), the cost of merging the two companies, and whether real customers show up.

Anthropic's $518 billion bill comes into view

Reuters reported September 29 that Anthropic's confidential IPO filing shows at least $518 billion in AI infrastructure commitments over about ten years — and roughly 80% of it largely can't be canceled. That's money it has promised to spend on computing power. It is not a new funding round, and it isn't money already spent.

Founder takeaway: Big commitments lock in capacity, but they also create pressure to earn enough to pay for it. Use the same rule at your size: before adding another AI subscription, write down the task, the benefit, and the monthly cost. If a tool fails that test, cancel it.

5 · Weekly Action Step

Run a 20-minute content-repurposing test

Pick one useful article, lesson, or video transcript you already made. Paste it into an AI tool you already pay for — no new subscription needed — and use this prompt:

Act as my content editor. Using only the material below, create three social posts and one short promotional email for [audience]. Each piece should address one specific problem and include one practical takeaway. Use a clear, conversational voice. Do not invent facts, testimonials, product features, or income claims. Mark missing information as [NEEDS INPUT]. End each piece with a relevant invitation to explore [offer]. Then give me a checklist of statements I should verify before publishing. Source material: [paste here]

Track three things:

  • ⏱ Total time
  • ✏️ Corrections needed
  • ✅ Pieces you'd actually post

Compare that with how you usually work. If it saves time without lowering quality, repeat it next week. If it doesn't, fix the instructions before you buy another tool.

Keep learning. Test one useful idea. Build on what works.
